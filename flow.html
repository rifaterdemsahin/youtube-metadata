Google Flow vs live-action 4.3× retention

The last three uploads are Google Flow animations (Veo). Lifetime average-percentage-viewed on the two scored Flow clips is 60%. The other 24 talking-head / screen-share videos sit at 14%. Length is a confound — and even against sub-8-minute live videos, Flow still holds ~3×.

Flow wins hold because the films are short, visual, and edited to a voiceover, not because Veo magically beats a 20-minute demo. Average Flow runtime is 1:05 vs 14:25 for live-action. That said, the six live videos already under 8 minutes only hold 19.3% — and the best of them (10x Interview Rate, 23.2%) is still less than half of Knowledge Engine (55.2%). Format is doing work that length alone does not explain.
Not just “shorts vs long”
Sub-8-minute live-action: 19.3% hold. Flow scored clips: 59.9%. Same order of magnitude as a bumper (11s, 64.6%) and a 69-second narrative (55.2%) — the hold stays high as Flow leaves bumper-length.

What this means for the next Flow film
  • 1. Keep the 60–90 second narrative. The 11-second bumper holds 65% because it is a bumper. The 69-second Knowledge Engine holding 55% is the real result — that is a story, not a sting.
  • 2. Do not “fair-fight” a 20-minute workshop against Flow on % viewed. Use this page for format decisions. Use August Analytics for watch-time hours. You need both: Flow for Suggested-training hold, live long-form for Search minutes.
  • 3. Recut the live champion into Flow. 10x Interview Rate already holds 23% as talking-head. A Flow 90-second sibling would test whether animation adds hold on a proven topic, not just on new topics.
  • 4. Wait on the Bubble film before changing the hook recipe. 28 views in ~40 hours is slower velocity than Knowledge Engine. Retention will decide if “AI isn’t a bubble” is a finishable argument on screen.
  • 5. Flow is not a substitute for packaging. August Browse is still 4 views. High hold only trains Suggested if the thumbnail wins the impression. Pair this page with CTR.
