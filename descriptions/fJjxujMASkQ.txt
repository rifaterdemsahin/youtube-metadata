Elon Musk just made a headline-grabbing prediction: within five years, as AI and humanoid robots scale, human labor becomes optional — maybe obsolete.

I'm arguing the opposite. Humans are going to have more jobs, not fewer. I've got two daughters growing up in this exact world, so this isn't academic for me — I pulled every argument I've got, not just the highlight reel.

Elon's right that productivity rises. Where he's wrong is what happens to us afterward. Every technological leap in history gets the same doom prediction — and every time, the Jevons Paradox kicks in: make a task cheaper, and demand for the output multiplies instead of the workforce shrinking.

The Economist's own tracking of the current AI boom shows US "hard-hat labour" running ~300,000 jobs above baseline by 2026, and white-collar STEM tracking 600,000–700,000 jobs above baseline over the same stretch. The AI boom isn't erasing jobs — it's creating them in hard hats and lab coats at the same time.
